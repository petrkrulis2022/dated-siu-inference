# Gate Market status — 2026-09-28

Point-in-time snapshot, taken right after the fourth redeploy (one-pool capacity accounting) and
after the three-window runner was built. Supersedes `status-2026-09-27.md`, which describes the
third trio and is now stale in its addresses and its sizing. This file goes stale the moment
another run happens — treat it as "state as of 2026-09-28 ~09:50 UTC", not a living document.

## What changed since yesterday

**Capacity is one pool now.** Before today, minting a dated work claim consumed an issuer's bonded
headroom and paying in USDC consumed nothing at all. That was found live in the first real P5
window: ISSUER-A's headroom sat untouched across an entire USDC purchase. It is wrong on its own
terms — the capacity is genuinely committed either way — and it also invalidates the experiment the
whole testbed exists to run, because an agent offered "claims or dollars" was really being offered
"accept a constraint or don't".

`WorkClaim` gained `reserveForWork`/`releaseReservation`:

- `reserveForWork(quoteHash, classId, quantity)` is callable **only by the seller named in a real,
  currently-Open `TouchstoneEscrow`** for that quote hash. Consuming headroom costs the caller
  nothing directly, so without that tie anyone could lock an issuer's whole pool for free. It
  routes through the same `ClaimRouter` a mint does, so a class with no headroom left reverts
  `NoIssuerWithHeadroom` at the moment of acceptance rather than after the work is done.
- `releaseReservation(quoteHash)` is **permissionless**, and allowed once the escrow settles or its
  own expiry passes. The permissionless abandoned-case release is the important half: an escrow
  opened, reserved against and then left alone must not lock an issuer's capacity indefinitely just
  because nobody with a stake bothers to call.

**What stays different between the two routes, and is disclosed rather than smoothed over:** a
claim reserves capacity for a *future* delivery window and is transferable; a dollar payment
consumes it for *immediate* work only and is not an instrument at all. The issuer is paid for a
claim and is **not** paid for a reservation — a real gap, left open deliberately rather than
papered over with a fee this testbed has no basis to set.

**The pool was resized to actually bind.** 60,000/40,000 mSIU per class became 24,000/16,000. The
old sizing could not produce the constraint the experiment is about: roughly 60,000 mSIU of
three-window demand against 100,000 mSIU of capacity. Lots are immutable (`createLot` reverts
`LotExists`) and `ClaimRouter` drains issuers in registration order, so resizing required a
redeploy regardless of the bytecode change.

## The next run: three windows, one pool

`packages/gate-market-agents/src/cli/p5-three-window-full-run.ts`, not yet run.

Scarcity is the instrument's only rationale here. The project's own series does not evidence the
deflation a forward curve would need — Commodity SIU drifted +0.034%/day at R²=0.21 over the
measured window — so nothing about a falling price makes a dated claim worth holding. What can is
that capacity for a later window is finite and someone else may take it first, and that is not
observable in a single window: there is no "later" to be shut out of.

**Scripted, and labelled as scripted:** that delegation happens (ORCHESTRATOR structurally lacks
the reference materials); that there are three windows and the agents are told so; and that a
simulated external buyer — the deployer wallet, not an agent, no model, no decisions — takes 14,000
mSIU after window 1 and 14,000 after window 2. Those numbers are fixed in the runner's source,
printed before window 1, and written into the manifest, so they cannot have been tuned to the
result.

**Not scripted:** which asset anyone pays in; whether an issuer states forward terms at all and at
what price; whether a buyer takes them, ignores them, or buys early to get ahead of the depletion;
and whether window 3 gets its work done.

**Claims can be dated for a later window, and that is what makes the run mean anything.** Until
today the loop always minted against the *current* window's bounds, so reserving capacity for a
future delivery window — the one property fSIU is defined by — was impossible, not merely untested.
An all-USDC result would then have read as a preference when the alternative it was compared
against never existed. `mint_claim`/`pay_with_claim` now take `forWindow`; a claim dated for a later
window consumes its issuer's headroom immediately and can only be presented once that window opens.
Windows are therefore fixed 20-minute spans computed from the chain clock before the first turn,
and the runner waits for each to open — a span that depends on when the previous window's agents
happen to finish is not knowable in advance. Proven on a real devnet before launch: a window-3
claim minted during window 1 consumes capacity at once, reverts `WindowNotOpenYet` if presented
early, and redeems normally once window 3 opens.

**Window 3 is allowed to fail, and the depletion is sized so the choice costs something.** 16,000
mSIU after window 1 and 14,000 after window 2. If the buyer never reserves ahead, window 3 opens
with 8,000/2,000 and no issuer can serve the 10,000 mSIU job — the scarcity finding. If it does
reserve ahead in window 1, ISSUER-A's 24,000 covers both that window's own job and a window-3
claim, the pool ends empty, and window 3 is delivered against the claim already held. Neither
branch is forced. The previous 14,000/14,000 sizing left 10,000 intact either way, so the decision
cost nothing; that is why it was changed. A claim routes to a *single* issuer with enough headroom
by itself, so the per-issuer split, not the total, is what a late buyer faces.

**The last window's three outcomes are reported apart**, since they otherwise read alike: nothing
reserved and the pool gone (the scarcity finding); capacity reserved or still available and the
window failed anyway (a defect, not a finding); capacity reserved and the window delivered against
it (the instrument working).

**One disclosed limitation.** No agent holds `settle_window_close` in this run, so an undelivered
claim stays outstanding — no bond payout, capacity never returned. Deliberate, and it can only make
scarcity tighter rather than looser. It is also currently unavoidable: settlement requires the
attested `printDate` to equal the claim's own window-close day, and the latest real commodity print
is dated the day before these windows close. Inventing a same-day print to make the mechanism
reachable is not a trade this project makes.

## Forward terms: a stated price, not an instrument

Issuers can now name their own number for the first time (`quote_forward`), and a buyer can record
acting on one (`take_forward`). Every offer is kept whether or not anyone takes it, alongside the
issuer's real headroom at the moment it quoted — an offer of 20,000 mSIU from an issuer holding 200
is a different datum from the same number backed by capacity.

**Nothing enforces any of it, and every surface that shows an offer says so.** A claim still mints
at the attested print rate, the attestation is publisher-signed, and no contract in this build could
bind an issuer to a price it named in advance. Making it binding means a new instrument, which build
1 does not have. What can be reported is what issuers offered and what buyers did about it — the raw
material a curve would be built from, and not itself a curve.

## Test state

and the conservation invariant rewritten as `outstanding == live claims + live reservations`, fuzzed
with both consumers moving at once). 262/262 in `@touchstone/gate-market-agents`, including two real

real rule, so a future change that quietly stops producing the scarcity finding fails a test rather
than a run.
